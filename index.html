01 / The critical problem

The problem is almost never the one everybody names.

It is not an absence of mission, of leadership, of programs, or of need.

Thousands of organizations in this country possess extraordinary access to human beings and cannot convert it into capital. An organization may be transforming lives every day while possessing no dedicated prospect research, no institutional proposal strategy, no outcome architecture, no financial positioning, and no time — because every hour of leadership is consumed maintaining what exists rather than capitalizing what could.

The critical problem is the absence of the infrastructure required to convert what an organization is already doing into an underwritable investment opportunity.

The result is a market failure of a peculiar and expensive kind. The community assets are real. The capacity to produce more of them is real. The capital that would pay for it is sitting in accounts right now, searching for somewhere defensible to go. And the three never meet, because nobody has stood in the gap and made the translation.

Money is not scarce in this country. Translation is.
